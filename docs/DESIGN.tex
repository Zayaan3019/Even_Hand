\section{What changed since the proposal}

Scope was approved unchanged, so this revision is driven by what we learned after 1 September.

\textbf{(a) The Institute prescribes no marks-to-grades rule.} The \emph{B.Tech Ordinances} (2015)
fix grades and points (R.21.1), make marking absolute (R.19.2) and leave letters to the Class
Committee (R.22.1, R.4.4(d)); no mean, standard deviation, top share or cut-off appears. So the
bands our proposal called institute policy are departmental convention, and the rule is per-course
configuration.

\textbf{(b) Grade points are unevenly spaced} (S=10, A=9, B=8, C=7, D=6, \textbf{E=4}, U=0; a U in
a core course must be repeated, R.25.1), so the shortlist ranks by expected grade-point change,
$P(\mathrm{grade\ changes})\times\Delta\,\mathrm{points}$, not by probability alone.

\textbf{(c) Severity harms a student only through \emph{differential exposure}}
(\S\ref{sec:audit}). When each question's marks of record come from one assistant, a harsh assistant
is indistinguishable from a harder question \emph{and moves nobody relative to anybody}. Pure
question-wise marking --- the proposal's ``failure'' case, and our stakeholder's arrangement --- is
therefore \emph{safe by design}, and the rotation we meant to recommend can \emph{create} the harm it
then has to measure. The plan changes accordingly (\S\ref{sec:milestones}).

\section{Architecture}

\subsection{One pipeline, nine modules, one seam}

A course enters at \texttt{eh-ingest} and leaves as a report from \texttt{eh-report};
\texttt{./mvnw verify} builds and tests all of it. Dependencies flow strictly downward, and
\texttt{eh-estimate}'s POM does not offer Spring, JPA or SQLite, so ``the estimator is tested
without a database'' is a property of the build graph. ArchUnit asserts the same rules;
\texttt{.github/CODEOWNERS} maps each module to its owner. $\dagger$: signature fixed here,
implementation planned.

\textbf{The seam.} Everything the measurement stage knows leaves it in one \texttt{FittedModel}
(estimates, covariance on the identified subspace, anchoring, verdict); nothing downstream reaches
back, so the estimator can be replaced without changing what the instructor is told. Its only entry
point takes an \texttt{AnchoredDesign}, whose single factory refuses a \textsc{not-estimable}
verdict or a certificate that is not true of the design in hand.

\subsection{Trust invariants}

Each is enforced by a type, the build graph or a named test --- never by convention.
\begin{enumerate}[label=I\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{No estimate without an audit of \emph{this} design} (\texttt{AnchoredDesign}), checked
at use because the audit runs weeks before the marks. The certificate is verified, not trusted: the
rank of the information matrix modulo two primes bounds its claimed rank from below, and a null
basis that every marking annihilates bounds it from above.
\item \textbf{No corrected mark ever becomes a grade.} Counterfactual marks stay inside
\texttt{eh-outcome}; the only per-student output is a recommendation to re-check a script, and a
grade changes only by a human re-mark (ArchUnit: no export path).
\item \textbf{Refusal is a value}: \textsc{not-estimable} stops estimation; a failed gate yields
\texttt{Shortlist.Refused(reason)}.
\item \textbf{Every figure carries its interval, every input its provenance} (measured, declared or
defaulted).
\item \textbf{No identifier on disk except in the key file, which is sealed first; no pseudonym
encodes order} (labels in keyed-digest order).
\item \textbf{Nothing leaves the machine}: loopback bind; ArchUnit forbids HTTP clients and sockets.
\item \textbf{Per-assistant figures reach only the instructor}; the committee sheet is aggregate.
\item \textbf{Reports reproduce byte-for-byte} from manifest and key: seeded RNG, sorted iteration,
\texttt{StrictMath}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The audit: who could have been harmed, and can it be measured?}
\label{sec:audit}

With $\eta_{nij}=\theta_n-\delta_i-\gamma_j$ (ability, difficulty, severity), the audit answers
three questions from the allocation alone. \textbf{It reads no marks}, so the instructor has the
answers before sharing any data; this is what we bring the stakeholder in week one.

\textbf{1. Exposure: could severity have moved anyone?} Let $E_{nj}$ be the share of student $n$'s
composite (question maxima $\times$ assessment weights, marks of record only) awarded by assistant
$j$. With one marker of record per question, each question's expected mark rises with ability under
the same assistant for everyone, so the expected composite rises with ability \emph{whatever the
severities}: rank-based grades are unchanged in expectation, and sigma bands move only as they
would for a harder paper. In general the harm is, to first order,
$\sum_j (E_{nj}-\bar E_j)\,s_{nj}\gamma_j$ with $s$ the local marks-per-logit slope: zero for
exposure-balanced rotation, largest for script-wise piles. Summed over assessments, it answers the
proposal's allocation-regime question before anything is estimated.

\textbf{2. Estimability: can what matters be separated?} For each pair of assistants we test
membership of the design's row space by fraction-free integer elimination --- a fact about the
arrangement, not a floating-point threshold (Facets' graph heuristic is documented to misreport
``exotic forms of connectedness''). A harm contrast $(E_n-E_m)\gamma$ is then estimable exactly when
every student carries the same weight inside every group of mutually comparable assistants.
Script-wise and question-wise marking are \emph{equally} rank-deficient (excess null dimension
$J{-}1$) but differ in kind. Question-wise, severity is confounded with difficulty and nothing about
students separates them. Script-wise, it is absorbed into the pile's abilities, and is recoverable if
the piles were alike. Re-asking against a design whose student columns are collapsed to one
intercept tells the two apart --- and that collapsed design is exactly the fixed-effect part of the
estimator's random-ability model (\S\ref{sec:est}), so the audit certifies what the estimator will
assume. Random and roll-number splits are the same design, so the audit reports evidence instead:
whether piles are contiguous in roll number or follow its department code, computed at ingest from
the real roll numbers and kept only as counts.

\textbf{3. Drift: does a late script lose?} The same test, with marking position as a column,
exposes a trap. If every marker works through the stack in the same (usually roll-number) order,
the same students are late on \emph{every} question, so the harm accumulates, yet drift is identified
only by assuming stack order is unrelated to ability. Having alternate markers reverse the stack
identifies drift outright and balances its exposure, at no cost. Under our stakeholder's arrangement,
drift, not severity, is the harm that can exist.

\textbf{The verdict} names the groups of mutually comparable assistants --- a partition, so two
equal-sized groups are both reported --- summarised as \textsc{estimable}, \textsc{under-exchangeability},
\textsc{partial} or \textsc{not-estimable}, and every verdict carries its exposure. Without
differential exposure the course is \textbf{safe} whatever the verdict, and \texttt{EH003} is a note,
not an error. With it, the harm is \textbf{correctable} if estimable (stated as resting on alike piles
when under exchangeability) and otherwise \textbf{harmful and unmeasurable}: the tool refuses and
names the repair.

\textbf{What changes in the advice.} The linking design optimises, in order: (1) no differential
exposure --- one marker of record per question, or rotation only in exposure-balanced form
(rotating over questions worth 5/10/15/10/10 marks leaves up to 5 of a student's 50 marks with a
different mix of assistants from the class's); (2) a small double-marked sample, which measures
inconsistency and anchors drift; (3) rotation for per-assistant feedback, only if the instructor
wants it. No within-course arrangement identifies the team's \emph{collective} severity; under an
absolute rule that needs the instructor to re-mark a sample, and the report says so.

\subsection{Estimation and uncertainty}
\label{sec:est}

\textbf{Model.} Many-facet Rasch, fitted by \emph{marginal} maximum likelihood (EM over
Gauss--Hermite quadrature, abilities a normal random effect; joint ML only for start values). Joint
ML was dropped as the estimator: its incidental-parameter bias needs a correction Facets leaves
manual; it offers no shrinkage at 60--150 students; and it cannot see that piles differ by chance ---
at 30 scripts per pile and an ability SD of 1 logit, pile means differ with SD 0.26 logits,
two-thirds of the proposal's own 0.4-logit example --- so its intervals under exchangeability would be
confidently wrong. Scores are modelled in the steps ingest reads; long scales use Andrich's
two-parameter dispersion thresholds, so a 20-mark question costs two parameters, not twenty.
Severity is per (assistant, session), with pooling tested by likelihood ratio. Erratic marking and
central tendency are reported, never folded into severity, and each question's effective weight in
the ranking is shown against its nominal weight.

\textbf{Uncertainty.} One engine pushes parameter draws (parametric bootstrap for headline figures)
through the composite (R.19.1), boundaries, grades and transfers, so every interval comes from one
tested component. \textbf{The no-effect test preserves the design}: it simulates the fitted
no-severity model on the \emph{same} allocation and refits. Permuting grader labels, as the proposal
planned, would turn a question-wise design into a crossed one and test the wrong null. The negative
control puts the real allocation's labels on an auto-scored component.

\subsection{Consequence, and privacy}

\texttt{GradingRule} is sealed (sigma-band, top-fraction, absolute, instructor-band; explicit
\texttt{sBandPolicy}). Counterfactual marks are referenced to the panel-average assistant, and
boundaries are recomputed on them. Hand-drawn bands have no knowable counterfactual, so we report
the range between holding the thresholds and holding the grade counts. The shortlist gate is
pre-registered in week 4: a shortlist is emitted only if the no-effect test rejects at 5\%; each row
needs $P(\mathrm{grade\ changes})\ge 0.2$; at most 20 rows, ranked by expected grade-point change,
with the expected number of false flags printed; rows run in both directions, and under a fixed
share each promotion names the script it displaces; every row carries the R.24.1 deadline.

\textbf{Privacy and security.} Threats: a lost laptop, re-identification, appraisal misuse,
exfiltration. Identifiers become HMAC-SHA-256 digests under a course-scoped salt, so a course's
assessments link but a new offering does not. Review found two gaps, both now fixed. Labels were
issued in file order, so in a roll-sorted export ``Student~1'' was the lowest roll number; they now
follow keyed-digest order. And the key file, the only re-identification table, was plain text; it
now carries the course salt and is sealed with AES-256-GCM under a passphrase (PBKDF2, 600,000
iterations, header authenticated; JDK only) before it is written. Pseudonyms do little for assistants
where the allocation is known (``the Q3 marker'' is a name), so per-assistant figures go only to the
instructor. Minimal fields, local processing and deletion on request follow the data-sharing note
and the DPDP Act, 2023.

numerics reach production. \textbf{104 tests pass today}, one of them a property checked on 150
random allocations, all in the four implemented modules; the other five have the signatures above and
no implementation yet, so their tests are \emph{planned}.

\section{Revised milestone plan}
\label{sec:milestones}

\begin{enumerate}[label=R\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{The audit moves ahead of the marks}, and already gives three answers from the
allocation alone: exposure, estimability and drift estimability. Week 1 runs it on the real one.
\item \textbf{A prediction replaces a risk.} If the allocation table confirms one marker of record
per question, we pre-register about zero severity-driven transfers on the first course. Drift joins
the core wherever marking order is recorded or can be declared.
\item \textbf{The second real course is requested in week 1, not week 7}, preferably script-wise,
where transfers are expected.
\item \textbf{Precision is the binding risk.} The design-only standard-error forecast gates the
shortlist; the mid-demo criterion is ``estimated, or refused with the repair named''.
\item \textbf{Marginal ML replaces JMLE plus manual bias correction.} Plan B is JMLE with pile means
constrained and pile variance added analytically, behind the same seam, chosen at the week-3 gate.
\item \textbf{Institutional fit}: configurable cut-off rule (\S1(a)), composites (R.19.1), and outputs
timed to the Class Committee and the R.24.1 window.
\item \textbf{Workload rebalanced.} Week 0's commits are one member's, and we say so. The seam lets
\texttt{eh-outcome} start on a synthetic fit, so Pruthviraj leads a deliverable every week and both
of us commit weekly.
\item \textbf{The cut line has dates}: question diagnostics on 2 Oct, the linking optimiser on 23 Oct,
drift only if no marking order exists. The audit, exposure analysis, estimator, propagation and blind
re-grade are never cut; the re-grade's pre-registration is committed in week 4.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Risks and plan B}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{The first course shows no severity harm.} Now expected and pre-registered (R2); a correct
``safe by design'' is a finding. Transfers are shown on the second course or, failing that, on the
stakeholder's own allocation with injected, known severities, labelled synthetic.
\item \textbf{Nobody recorded who marked what}, as is usual with paper scripts. Intake will accept the
allocation as rules (``TA~2: Q3--Q4, all students''; ``TA~1: all questions, CE23B001--060''), with a
template to use before the next exam is marked.
\item \textbf{Marking order was not recorded.} The instructor may declare roll-number order (flagged
\textsc{declared}); recording it at the end-semester exam costs nothing.
\item \textbf{Intervals are too wide, or marginal ML slips.} We report the intervals and refuse the
shortlist, as a type; the estimator falls back to plan B (R5) at the week-3 gate.
\item \textbf{No effect is detected.} We report it, with the effect we can rule out and the sample
needed; we do not shop for another course.
\item \textbf{An assistant's standing is harmed.} Per-assistant figures go to the instructor only;
erratic marking is reported apart from harsh; estimates describe a marking session, not a person.
\item \textbf{The blind re-grade is not blind}: returned scripts carry the assistant's ink, so scans
are masked before they go back (else ten flagged and ten control scripts, else a reported gap). The
test is directional, with its power pre-registered.
\end{enumerate}
