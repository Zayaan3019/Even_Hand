\section{What changed since the proposal}

Scope was approved unchanged, so this document revises the proposal on facts learned since
1 September rather than on requested cuts.

\textbf{(a) The Institute prescribes no marks-to-grades rule.} We read the official
\emph{B.Tech Ordinances and Regulations} (2015). R.21.1 fixes the grade set and points; R.19.2
states markings are on an \emph{absolute} basis; R.22.1 with R.4.4(d) place the awarding of
letters with the Class Committee meeting held within seven days of the last end-semester exam.
\textbf{No mean, standard deviation, fixed top share or cut-off rule appears anywhere} --- the
only ``cut-off'' text concerns JEE admission. The familiar relative bands are departmental
convention, and published accounts of them contradict one another. Our proposal called them
institute policy; that was wrong, and correcting it strengthens the design: \emph{the cut-off
rule cannot be compiled in, because the Institute delegates it per course.}

\textbf{(b) Grade points are unevenly spaced, so harm is not uniform across boundaries.} R.21.1
gives S=10, A=9, B=8, C=7, D=6, \textbf{E=4}, U=0 --- there is no 5. A script misplaced across
B/C costs one grade point; across E/U it costs four, and under R.25.1 a U in a core course must
be repeated. We therefore rank the shortlist by \emph{expected grade-point loss},
$P(\mathrm{grade\ changes})\times\Delta\,\mathrm{points}$, not by probability alone. This splits
the proposal's prediction into two testable halves: \emph{transfers} should cluster at B/C and
A/B where student density peaks, while \emph{damage} clusters at D/E and E/U.

\textbf{(c) Computing rank rather than connectivity showed the two marking arrangements fail
differently, and only one is recoverable} (\S\ref{sec:audit}).

\section{Architecture}

\subsection{The pipeline is a compiler}

The first output is not an estimate but a verdict on what the data can support. That is a static
analysis, and the system is built as one: it rejects ill-formed input before the expensive stage,
names the construct at fault, and proposes a rewrite.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{|l|l|}
\hline
\textbf{Compiler stage} & \textbf{Even Hand} \\
\hline
Lexer / parser & \texttt{eh-ingest} --- CSV to typed responses; diagnostics carry row numbers \\
Symbol table / IR & \texttt{eh-core} --- the marking design as an incidence structure \\
\textbf{Semantic analysis} & \textbf{\texttt{eh-design} --- the identifiability audit; refuses ill-formed designs} \\
Fix-it hint & \texttt{eh-linking} --- the smallest repair that would make it well-formed \\
Code generation & \texttt{eh-estimate} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{eh-outcome} --- runs only on a design that type-checked \\
Runtime & \texttt{eh-app} --- Spring Boot, Thymeleaf, SQLite, bound to 127.0.0.1 \\
\hline

\subsection{The seam between measurement and consequence}

Everything the measurement stage knows leaves it in one record, \texttt{FittedModel}: the three
parameter vectors, their standard errors, the anchoring used, the parameter covariance, and the
audit's verdict. Nothing downstream reaches back into how the fit was computed. That matters
practically rather than for tidiness: the estimator is the part most likely to be replaced --- a
different anchoring, a rating-scale model instead of partial credit, a Bayesian fit if the
intervals prove too wide at this class size --- and confining what it exports means such a change
cannot quietly alter what the instructor is told.

The estimator's entry point takes an \texttt{AnchoredDesign}, which has a private constructor and
one factory, \texttt{AnchoredDesign.of(design, verdict, anchoring)}. It refuses a
\texttt{NotEstimable} verdict, and refuses any verdict whose certificate does not carry
\emph{this} design's fingerprint. An unaudited fit is therefore not something we remember not to
write; it is something that cannot be written, and a clean verdict earned on one course cannot be
presented as evidence about another.

The check sits at the point of use rather than inside the audit because the two do not run
together. The audit needs no marks, so it runs as soon as the allocation table exists --- often
weeks before there is anything to estimate --- and its verdict must survive until then. By that
time the marking may have changed: a script re-marked, a row added, a fresh export produced. So
the audit emits a \emph{certificate} (rank, excess null dimension, null basis, fingerprint) and
the estimator re-verifies it. The verdict is evidence rather than an assertion, and a stale one is
caught where it is used instead of silently licensing an estimate on marking nobody audited.

\subsection{Nothing about a course is compile-time}

Every course-shaped fact is \emph{inferred} by the profiler (arrangement per assessment,
double-marked units, score type, whether piles look like roll-number blocks), or \emph{asked once}
on a first-login intake screen pre-filled with the profiler's guess, or \emph{defaulted} and
flagged unconfirmed. Each \texttt{CourseProfile} field carries its \texttt{Provenance}
(\textsc{detected}/\textsc{declared}/\textsc{defaulted}) and evidence, and every report footer
states which inputs were measured and which asserted, so a disputed conclusion traces to the
assumption under it. No control flow branches on the arrangement; the label is for the report
only. The complete list of remaining assumptions, each with its override, is in
\texttt{docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md}.

\subsection{The identifiability audit, and what it found}
\label{sec:audit}

With $\eta_{nij}=\theta_n-\delta_i-\gamma_j$, adding a constant to every ability and every
difficulty changes nothing, and so does adding one to every ability and every severity. That
two-dimensional indeterminacy is in every design and is what anchoring removes; dimensions beyond
it are real confounds. Rather than measure the null space and interpret it, we ask the
instructor's own question once per pair of assistants --- \emph{is the difference between these
two severities estimable?} --- which is the question of whether the vector expressing it lies in
the row space of the design. Rank is computed over the integers by fraction-free elimination, so
the verdict is a fact about the arrangement, not a consequence of a floating-point threshold; it
is computed again from the eigenvalues of the Fisher information and the two must agree on every
fixture. Facets uses a graph-joining heuristic after Weeks and Williams (1964) whose manual states
that ``there are exotic forms of connectedness which Facets may falsely report as disconnected'',
and meets the nested case by asking the user to \emph{declare} their way out of it. We measure
whether the data escapes it, and if not, compute what would.

\textbf{The finding.} Script-wise and question-wise marking are \emph{equally} rank-deficient ---
both have excess null dimension $J{-}1$ for $J$ assistants, so neither identifies a single
severity difference --- but they fail differently. Under question-wise marking severity is
confounded with \emph{question difficulty}, and no assumption about students touches it, because
no student was marked on that question by anyone else. Under script-wise marking severity is
absorbed into the \emph{abilities of the students in that pile}, which \emph{is} recoverable by
assuming the piles were alike --- what random allocation buys. The audit separates the two by
re-asking the same question against a design whose student columns are collapsed to one intercept;
collapsing them \emph{is} the exchangeability assumption. Hence four verdicts, not two.

Two consequences. A limitation, stated rather than hidden: a random split and a fixed roll-number
split are \emph{the same design of the same rank}, so the audit cannot distinguish them and does
not pretend to --- it reports whether each pile is a contiguous block of roll numbers as evidence
for the reader. And a finding worth acting on: \textbf{question-wise marking in which the assigned
marker rotates across blocks of roll numbers is fully identified with no double-marking at all}
(Appendix A, row 2). Nobody marks an extra script. The linking design therefore proposes rotation
\emph{before} second marking, because an instructor told to reorder work they were already doing
will comply at a different rate from one told to add work.

\textbf{The audit reads no marks.} It is a function of the arrangement alone, so an instructor can
be told what his data supports before sharing any of it. This takes data access off the critical
path (\S\ref{sec:milestones}, R1) and is the artefact we bring the stakeholder in week one.

\subsection{Estimation, uncertainty, outcome, privacy}

Many-facet Rasch by joint maximum likelihood (PROX start, alternating Newton updates, re-centring
to the anchoring), partial-credit thresholds where scores are not binary, and a documented
category-collapsing rule. Standard errors from the full observed information,
generalised-inverted on the identified subspace. Facets documents its JMLE bias correction as a
\emph{manual} procedure; since we need the synthetic generator anyway, we automate it. Severity is
estimated per (assistant, session); pooling across sessions is tested by likelihood ratio, not
assumed. \emph{Erratic} markers are reported separately from \emph{harsh} ones, because conflating
them would make an output unsafe to show an assistant.

\textbf{One uncertainty engine drives every interval}: it draws from the parameter covariance on
the identified subspace and pushes each draw through the whole deterministic downstream ---
re-weighting the composite (R.19.1 makes a course grade a weighted composite whose parts may have
been marked under different arrangements), recomputing boundaries, assigning grades, counting
transfers. Transfer counts, the grader share of class spread, and the shortlist's
$P(\mathrm{grade\ changes})$ come from one component tested once. The permutation test is separate
and does refit, giving the null distribution of severity spread and the minimum effect detectable
at 80\% power.

\texttt{GradingRule} is sealed, with sigma-band, top-fraction, absolute and instructor-band
implementations and an explicit \texttt{sBandPolicy}, because \S1(a) shows no canonical convention
exists. Boundaries are recomputed on corrected marks. The shortlist is capped, ranked by expected
grade-point loss, carries the R.24.1 revision deadline on every row, and \emph{refuses to emit}
when the intervals cannot support it. Identifiers are HMAC-hashed with a per-run salt at ingest;
only the digest reaches SQLite, the salt lives in the instructor's key file which the application
never reads back, and a test scans the database bytes to prove it. Every run writes a manifest
that reproduces its report byte-for-byte.

numerics reach the production classpath and the estimation code stays examinable. Categories:
unit, property-based, statistical calibration (seeded), scenario matrix, golden snapshots,
integration, privacy, performance, negative control. 47 tests pass today.

\section{Revised milestone plan}
\label{sec:milestones}

\begin{enumerate}[label=R\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{The audit moves ahead of the marks.} It is a function of the design, not the scores,
so week 1 delivers a verdict and precision forecast with no marks in hand. Removes the project's
largest dependency from the critical path.
\item \textbf{Build order re-prioritised, design not narrowed.} The first course is question-wise,
so the refusal path, bridge analysis and linking design move to weeks 1--2. Other arrangements
stay green in the scenario matrix.
\item \textbf{Precision, not identifiability, is the binding risk} at 60--150 students and 3--5
assistants. The design-only standard-error forecast becomes a deliverable and gates whether a
shortlist is produced. Mid-demo criterion becomes ``severity estimated \emph{or} correctly refused
with the repair specified''.
\item \textbf{Severity estimated per marking session}, pooling tested by likelihood ratio ---
required by the mixed arrangement used for assignments.
\item \textbf{New: the cut-off rule is unregulated configuration, not institute policy}
(\S1(a)). Adds the intake flow, \texttt{sBandPolicy}, and a week-1 question to the stakeholder.
\item \textbf{New: composite multi-assessment scoring in scope from the start} (R.19.1).
\item \textbf{New: outputs aim at the Class Committee meeting and the R.24.1 window} --- a
one-page committee sheet, a 60-second runtime budget, the revision deadline on every shortlist
row, and the linking design retargeted from ``next semester'' to the post-Quiz-II committee
meeting for this course's own end-semester exam (R.4.4(a)).
\item \textbf{The cut line gets dates, not just an order.} Question diagnostics cut if the week-3
gate misses (2 Oct), drift if week-5 misses (16 Oct), linking design if week-6 misses (23 Oct).
The audit, estimator, propagation and blind re-grade are never cut. The blind re-grade's
pre-registration is committed to git in week 4, before any real estimate exists.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Risks and plan B}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{The first course is question-wise, so severity may be inseparable from question
difficulty.} Now the expected case, not a tail risk, so \emph{plan B is already the main line}:
the audit reports it as a verdict, names the assistants affected, and specifies the repair. The
double-marked scripts the stakeholder confirmed exist are the live mitigation, and week 1 settles
on the allocation table alone whether they are enough. If not, the first course's audit is a
result in its own right and the case study moves to a course whose arrangement supports
estimation.
\item \textbf{Precision, not identifiability, is the likeliest failure.} Severities may be
identified but with intervals too wide to act on. We report the intervals and \emph{refuse to
produce a shortlist the estimates cannot support} --- implemented as a type, not a promise. A
system that declines when the data is thin is a better result than one that answers anyway.
\item \textbf{The marks have not arrived.} Mitigated structurally by R1: the audit, the precision
forecast and the linking design all run on an allocation table with no scores, so a delay costs us
the estimate and not the project.
\item \textbf{The cut-off convention we assumed may not be his.} \S1(a): the Institute prescribes
none and public accounts disagree. Every policy ships behind configuration, labelled unconfirmed
until he confirms it; only the default is at stake.
\item \textbf{The permutation test comes back null.} A legitimate outcome, treated as one. We
report the null, the effect size we can rule out, and the sample that would have been needed. We
will not go looking for a different course until we find a positive result, and we say so.
\item \textbf{Severity estimates could damage an assistant's standing.} Pseudonymised at ingest
with only the instructor holding the key; no output ranks named individuals; every estimate
carries its interval; erratic markers reported separately from harsh ones; the framing throughout
is calibration of a marking session, not judgement of a person.
\item \textbf{He declines the blind re-grade.} Negotiate down to ten flagged and ten control
scripts in advance; if he declines entirely we report the missing experiment as a gap rather than
substituting a weaker proxy.
\end{enumerate}
